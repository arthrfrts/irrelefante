Gostei muito [desse ensaio de Lisa Russ Spaar][lrs] para o _The American Scholar_ em que ela traça as semelhanças entre a poética e a perda --- como um esmiuça e registra talvez o mais básico dos sentimentos, o de perceber a ausência:

> A poem is a mediation. It conjures up and stands in for what is not present: a woman much missed, a summer’s day, a Grecian urn, woods on a snowy evening. In his poem “Meditation at Lagunitas,” Robert Hass writes, “The word is elegy to what it signifies.” In Lyric Time: Dickinson and the Limits of Genre, Sharon Cameron writes that “language, then, is loss’s consequence. Loss gives birth to language in the strenuous aftermath of its own labor of grief at the price exacted by social existence. … [Loss is] also, independently, the first principle of the phenomenal world.”
>
> […]
>
> We lose. We’re sundered. We will die. December thins the herd. December becomes January. Years end. Years commence. […] And with each passing year that we’re privileged to live, we will, as Bishop says, lose _farther_ (why do I read “father” here?), lose _faster_.
